# Table I - Comparative Analysis of Related Literature and the Proposed Framework

(Reproduced verbatim from Section IV of the paper. Narrative/qualitative
table, not a computational result — included here for completeness only.)

| Author [Ref] | Focus Area | Key Limitation Identified/Research Gap | Contribution of This Proposed Work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gramegna & Giudici [14] | SHAP vs. LIME comparison analysis | Does not address a financially excluded or credit invisible population | Employs SHAP explicitly to a thin-file-style applicant profile for transparent explanations |
| Bussmann et al. [6] | XAI in SME credit risk assessment | Uses proprietary, non-reproducible lending data | Uses a fully public, reproducible dataset and open-source pipeline |
| Nallakaruppan et al. [7] | XAI framework for credit evaluation | General framework, no India/financial-inclusion framing | Frames the problem explicitly around India's credit-invisible population |
| Addy et al. [10] | Review and survey of AI techniques in credit scoring | Survey only; no original experiment | Provides an original benchmark experiment plus SHAP interpretability analysis |
| Hlongwane et al. [11] | Shapley-based interpretable credit scorecards | No comparison against black-box ensemble baseline models | Directly compares interpretable and black-box models on the same dataset |
